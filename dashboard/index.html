Why Zerone exists

What matters most is that there is a record.
Then everyone can talk it through.

Corrections usually vanish.

When a claim turns out to be wrong, the fix often replaces it and the path is lost. Zerone keeps the earlier claim beside its correction, so the next reader can see what changed and why.

One keeper can quietly rewrite.

A record held by one party can be edited without anyone noticing. Zerone is a blockchain: it links each block of records to the one before it, so anyone holding an earlier copy can tell if kept history was changed.

Readers come and go.

People come and go, and so do AI models. What is kept lets the next reader, human or agent, pick up the reasoning and carry it on.

How it works, by design

  1. Someone makes a claim with its scope, method and evidence, and signs it with their own key.
  2. Others review it. Each reviewer votes in secret first, then reveals the vote, so no one can copy another. The result records what the reviewers said. It does not prove the claim is true.
  3. Anyone with an account can challenge it later by putting down a ZRN deposit. Challenges and corrections are added beside the original, never written over it.
  4. Money stays apart from truth. ZRN, the chain’s token, pays its fees and deposits. Holding ZRN, or being paid, never makes a claim true.

Today one household, the people who started Zerone, runs the live chain, zerone-1. It runs an older version of these steps, without some of the protections above. On 20 October 2026, every entry it showed as verified had been written in when the chain began, without review; the 25 claims accepted after review are no longer marked verified, and none has been accepted since 4 August 2026. A separate test chain, with valueless test funds, tries a newer version from September 2026; no chain runs the whole design yet. Understand Zerone walks through an example, and what you can do today is below.

Today, honestly

Zerone is live. Control is not yet shared.

One household, the people who started Zerone, runs the only computer that makes blocks and holds the vote on every change. That household can stop the chain, roll it back, or restart it from scratch.

  • By 20 October 2026 the chain had stopped twice for more than a day: about 28 hours in July 2026 and about 54 hours in August 2026. Both times, blocks started again. It has also paused for shorter spells, from a couple of minutes to about half an hour.
  • The same household holds nearly all ZRN: at least 95% on 20 October 2026, counting only its two founding accounts and its stake. Votes on changes to the chain are weighted by staked ZRN, and all staked ZRN is the household’s. Both accounts are linked under Check it yourself. There is no market for ZRN on Zerone, and no price is promised.
  • The live numbers on this page are read from the household’s own node, through this site. They are an observation, not an independent proof.
  • The live chain runs older code than Zerone’s current source. New code reaches it through upgrades; the last ran on 25 July 2026, and the planned ones have not run yet.
  • Today the household offers no new accounts on zerone-1 and no way to join it as a validator: there is no sign-up, no starter ZRN, and no supported joining path. The source code is not public.

What you can rely on: the record is public and read live. Each block carries the hash of the one before it, so if you note a block’s height and full hash today (Check it yourself shows how), you can later see whether the history up to it was changed. That shows a rewrite; it does not prevent one. The aim is for independent operators to run validators they alone control. That has not happened yet, and no date is promised.

    Delete subject-linked pilot data?

    This ends every Zerone Pi pilot session associated with the current app-specific subject and removes its active address proof and outstanding challenges. This cannot be undone from this session.

    What is deleted

    Subject-linked session usernames and token digests, active Zerone address binding, outstanding proof challenges, their challenge-request rate records, and subject-key aliases used during pepper rotation.

    What can remain separately or in provider recovery

    A keyed subject guard blocks older in-flight authorization for exactly 12 minutes; its row may remain until operator cleanup. Minimal bearer replay commitments are not subject-linked or selectable by this account action. Promoted markers are not eligible for ordinary cleanup and may be retained indefinitely unless a separately approved purge policy replaces that rule. Account-unlinked challenge tombstones remain behind separate security-retention controls. They contain no raw Pi UID, username, bearer, wallet address, or KYC data. Logical deletion may also remain provider-recoverable for up to 30 days on a Workers Paid plan or seven days on a Workers Free plan; the deployed plan decides the window.
