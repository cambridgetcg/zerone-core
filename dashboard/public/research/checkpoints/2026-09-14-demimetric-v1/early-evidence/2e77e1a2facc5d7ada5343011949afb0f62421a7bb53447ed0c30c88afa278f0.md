# Case plan: inspect one operator-property inference

**Result:** a useful, bounded proof-step lead, with an exact algebraic counterexample to the stated nonexpansiveness inference. The convergence conclusion of Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4 has **not** been refuted or fully audited. This investigation is separate from the publisher's peer-review-process concern; it is not evidence of misconduct or a novelty claim.

Original: Hammad, Dafaalla and Abdalla, “Creating a novel algorithm for studying the strong convergence to a sequence with applications,” PLOS One (2025), DOI **10.1371/journal.pone.0319047**. Retained original HTML SHA256 `20005d5fad78974a5b0acf4fb1e45d383c04a5e6f3be605236d81ce81b2f4e0f`.

[Definition 2.3](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0319047#article1.body1.sec2.p6), [Lemma 2.7](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0319047#article1.body1.sec2.p15), [Lemma 3.2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0319047#article1.body1.sec3.p4), and especially the [Lemma 3.3 proof](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0319047#article1.body1.sec3.p14) are the selected scope. In the last location the proof moves from demimetricity to nonexpansiveness of the weighted relaxed operator Z. Lemma 2.7(3) itself gives the weaker quasi-nonexpansiveness property.

Nineteen small original-publisher equation images were therefore fetched, inspected over white backgrounds, and retained under `case-equations/` with source URLs, hashes and publisher storage paths. No other paper or removed content was fetched. Cited predecessors were not inspected.

The separate [PLOS expression of concern](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0343283) reports peer-review-process concerns; it does not assert this mathematical issue. Its retained HTML SHA256 is `86d29b02ce1bb2d4a6ed274b466c0e6a07b4dad7410d49d4c87ec436e42b4723`.

## Falsifiable check and outcome

Question: do the printed positive generalized-demimetric condition, residual demiclosedness, positive relaxation less than one, and even a normalized finite average imply that Z is nonexpansive?

Use the explicit continuous scalar Q in `CASE-MANUAL-DERIVATION.md`, two identical copies, weights 1/2, and relaxation b=1/2 with lambda_1=3/4. Q satisfies the printed generalized condition with parameter phi=1 and has the sole fixed point 0. Its residual is demiclosed at zero. Nevertheless Z sends 1 to 1/2 and 3/2 to 5/4: output separation 3/4 exceeds input separation 1/2. These are exact rational calculations, not a numerical search or simulation. The fully visible fixed-point identity in Lemma 2.7(1) passes a separate algebraic control.

## What remains open

The source's weight and index notation is inconsistent: e138 is a finite weighted sum, e079 in Lemma 2.10 prints an infinite sum equal to infinity, and e163 prints an infinite sum of a differently named symbol equal to one. The check does not silently correct these expressions or claim a full instance of every global algorithm hypothesis. Its normalized two-map average deliberately gives the questioned implication the usual favorable interpretation.

A next proof audit should transcribe the complete Lemma 3.3 hypotheses, distinguish original maps from their averaged operator, and identify whether any additional stated assumption actually forces nonexpansiveness. If not, try replacing that invocation with an argument using residual demiclosedness and fixed-point inequalities. Such a repair may preserve the convergence result; it must be proved, not presumed. Stop or report inconclusive if the notation cannot be resolved from authoritative source context. A theorem counterexample would require satisfying every theorem hypothesis while violating its conclusion; this check does neither claim nor supply that.
