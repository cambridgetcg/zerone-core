# Exact manual derivation

## Printed condition and chosen operator

Definition 2.3(b), equation image **e505**, gives the generalized-demimetric condition

    phi * <x-p, x-Q(x)> >= ||x-Q(x)||^2,

for fixed points p and nonzero phi. The section-3 parameter image e085 restricts phi_j to positive values. Work in the real Hilbert space R and set

    Q(x) = 0         if x <= 1,
           2*x - 2   if 1 < x < 3/2,
           1         if x >= 3/2.

This is a constructed test operator, not an example claimed to appear in the article. It is continuous at both breakpoints (the adjacent values are respectively 0 and 1).

## Fixed points and the generalized condition

On x<=1, Q(x)=x holds only at x=0. On the middle interval the equation would give x=2, outside that interval. On x>=3/2 it would give x=1, again outside the interval. Thus Fix(Q)={0}.

Write r=x-Q(x). For x<=1, r=x, so x*r=r^2. In the middle interval r=2-x, and 0<r<x. For x>=3/2, r=x-1, again 0<r<x. Consequently x*r>=r^2 for every real x. With p=0 and **phi=1**, this proves exactly the inequality displayed in e505.

The residual I-Q is continuous. In R, weak and strong sequential convergence coincide; hence if x_n converges weakly to x and (I-Q)x_n tends to zero, continuity gives (I-Q)x=0. This verifies residual demiclosedness at zero under the natural operator reading of the section-3 assumption. It does not infer a global pairwise Lipschitz bound from demiclosedness.

## Relaxation and finite average

Images **e101/e103/e104** state V_j=(1-b)I+bQ_j and 0<b<lambda_1<1. Choose Q_1=Q_2=Q, b=1/2 and lambda_1=3/4. Both relaxed maps are V=(I+Q)/2. The finite weighted operator shown in **e138** becomes Z=(V_1+V_2)/2=V with positive weights 1/2 and 1/2.

All fixed points remain 0. For x>=0 the value Q(x) lies between 0 and x, so V(x) does too. For x<0, V(x)=x/2. Therefore |V(x)-0|<=|x-0| for every x: V is quasi-nonexpansive relative to its fixed point.

But the stronger all-pairs condition fails:

    Q(1)=0,          Z(1)=1/2,
    Q(3/2)=1,        Z(3/2)=5/4,
    |Z(3/2)-Z(1)| = 3/4 > 1/2 = |3/2-1|.

Thus Z is not nonexpansive. More generally these same inputs give separation (1+b)/2 for every b>0, while their original separation is 1/2. Relaxation alone cannot repair this implication.

## Positive control: Lemma 2.7(1)

For any map Q on the same domain and V=(1-b)I+bQ with b nonzero,

    V(x)=x  iff  b*(Q(x)-x)=0  iff  Q(x)=x.

This establishes the fixed-point equality stated there, including for the constructed example. It illustrates why a valid fixed-point identity and quasi-nonexpansiveness do not imply pairwise nonexpansiveness.

## Exact limitation

The counterexample falsifies the identified operator-property inference under the reconstructed local assumptions, including a favorable normalized finite average. It is not a counterexample to the conclusion of Lemma 3.3 or Theorem 3.4. Their full hypotheses, sequence construction, notation ambiguities, and possible repair through the original maps' demiclosedness remain outside this check. There is no claim of discovering this distinction first, no empirical ML replication, and no connection inferred to the reason for the editorial notice.
